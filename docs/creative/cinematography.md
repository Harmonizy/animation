# Cinematography

**Status:** cameras with position, zoom and rotation, plus shots and cuts, are implemented. Everything
else here is **Planned** vocabulary. These are conventions, not fixed rules: break them on purpose.
Sources: [references](../REFERENCES.md).

## Shot sizes

| Size             | Typical use                               |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Extreme wide     | Place, scale, isolation                   |
| Wide             | Subject within the environment            |
| Full             | Whole body, posture and movement          |
| Medium           | Conversation and gesture                  |
| Medium close-up  | Attention on the person with some context |
| Close-up         | Emotion or a significant detail           |
| Extreme close-up | Intensity, or a detail seen as a clue     |

## Camera angles

- **Eye level:** neutral, the most relatable.
- **Low angle:** often gives size or power.
- **High angle:** often gives smallness or exposure.
- **Overhead:** pattern, distance, a map-like view.
- **Dutch angle:** unease.
- **Point of view:** seeing as a character sees.
- **Over-the-shoulder:** connects two people and their positions.

The meaning of an angle depends on context, and the associations above are tendencies only.

## Camera movement

- **Static:** stillness, observation.
- **Pan and tilt:** rotation that reveals or follows.
- **Dolly:** moving toward or away changes the relationship with the subject.
- **Tracking:** moving alongside a subject.
- **Orbit:** circling, which emphasizes a subject or moment.
- **Zoom:** changes framing without moving the camera, and looks different from a dolly in real optics.

Every movement should have a motivation. In this 2D system, dolly and zoom look alike until parallax
exists.

## Composition

Rule of thirds, negative space, leading lines, symmetry, visual balance, foreground/midground/background
layers, depth cues and subject framing all direct the eye. Check them in every target aspect ratio,
since a composition that works at 16:9 may fail at 9:16.

## Continuity

- **180-degree rule:** keep the camera on one side of the action to preserve screen direction.
- **Screen direction:** consistent movement keeps geography clear.
- **Eyeline match:** a look followed by what is being looked at.
- **Match on action:** cut during a movement to hide the cut.
- **Cutaways:** a separate detail that gives information or time.
- **Reaction shots:** often carry more emotion than the event itself.
