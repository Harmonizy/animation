The TypeScript is the source of truth. The SVG files are generated by `npm run build:harmonie`, and a
unit test fails if the committed files differ from what the code produces. To change the character,
edit the geometry, rebuild, look at the result, and commit both. Editing the SVG by hand is fine for
sketching an idea, but the change only becomes real once it is moved into `harmonie-geometry.ts`.

Open the preview with `?view=harmonie`, or pick "Character: Harmonie (CHR-001)" in the view menu.

## Art direction

**Shape language.** Long vertical lines and one point. The hood comes to a soft point, slightly off
centre towards her left. Everything below it falls in an A-line: hood, shoulder mantle, cloak. Curves
are gentle and corners are few and deliberate (hood tip, mantle hem, cloak hem corners, belt), which
keeps the figure calm rather than aggressive.

**Read order.** 1. The pointed hood and dark face opening. 2. The amber scarf, the only warm color. 3. The asymmetric cloak hem. 4. The teal tunic, belt and satchel as secondary detail.

**Controlled asymmetry.** Three elements are deliberately one-sided, all fixed to her own left or right:

- the cloak hem is longer on her right (745 against 690);
- the scarf tail hangs on her left;
- the satchel sits on her right hip.

The right shoulder mantle is also a few units longer than the left, echoing the hem. Nothing else is
asymmetric, so these read as design and not as error.

**Faceless.** The face is one Deep Shadow shape inside the hood. It has no features and never gets
any. Emotion comes from the spine, head angle, arm space and stance width only.

**Conventions.** Left and right always mean Harmonie's own. From the front her left is on screen
right. The side view is her **left** profile, facing screen left, so the scarf tail is on the near
side. The rig is not mirrored to face the other way, because mirroring would swap her costume's sides.
A right profile would need its own drawing (planned, not built).

## Palette

| Token        | HEX       | Used for                                                                  |
| ------------ | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `deepShadow` | `#202732` | Face and inner hood, undersleeves, gloves, trousers, boots, belt, satchel |
| `slateBlue`  | `#405567` | Hood shell, cloak, shoulder mantle                                        |
| `mistTeal`   | `#789A9C` | Inner tunic, body and skirt                                               |
| `amberGold`  | `#E2B66B` | Scarf, and the satchel buckle as the one other accent                     |
| `warmIvory`  | `#E8E3D8` | Sheet and preview background. Never on the character                      |

No other colors, and no gradients. Three decisions needed justification:

1. **Sleeves are Deep Shadow, not Mist Teal.** With teal sleeves, the near arm vanished against the
   teal tunic in the side view and every pose that crossed the body. Treating them as a dark
   underlayer, like the trousers, keeps the arms readable on both the tunic and the cloak.
2. **The face and the inner hood share Deep Shadow.** The opening reads as one shadowed void, which is
   what a deep hood does, and the face shape alone defines it.
3. **Sheet and preview chrome** (grid, frames, labels, the preview's ground line) use Deep Shadow at
   reduced opacity. That is a technical aid on the sheet, not part of the character.

## Proportions

The figure is 800 units tall, from the top of the head room (y = 0) to the soles (y = 800), with
x = 0 on the centre line. The baseline from the brief was kept, with these refinements:

| Part   | Brief Y | Built Y | Why                                                                     |
| ------ | ------- | ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Head   | 55–178  | 55–178  | Unchanged. It is a construction ellipse, mostly hidden by the hood      |
| Hood   | 25–190  | 22–202  | The tip rose 3 units to read as a point; the base tucks under the scarf |
| Neck   | 175–220 | 172–222 | Overlaps head and torso so no gap opens when the head turns             |
| Torso  | 205–475 | 206–476 | Unchanged                                                               |
| Pelvis | 455–525 | 456–536 | The tunic skirt, carried by the pelvis, falls to 598                    |
| Arms   | 230–570 | 224–557 | Shoulder 240, elbow 398, wrist 548. Round joint caps add a few units    |
| Hands  | 540–590 | 542–590 | Unchanged                                                               |
| Legs   | 510–765 | 493–767 | Hip 512, knee 636, ankle 758. Round joint caps add a few units          |
| Boots  | 735–800 | 734–800 | The shaft flares past the shin so the boot reads in silhouette          |
| Cloak  | 205–745 | 198–745 | The mantle starts at the collar, under the scarf                        |
| Scarf  | 190–530 | 192–532 | Wrap 192–242, knot, tail to 532                                         |

Additions beyond the brief: a **shoulder mantle** in Slate Blue, which joins hood and cloak into one
garment and gives the silhouette a step at the shoulders; and the **tunic skirt**, so the pelvis has
visible cloth that moves with it.

Joint heights are shared by every view. The views differ only in horizontal positions and part shapes.

## Turnaround

Four views are drawn separately, not rotated from one drawing:

- **Front:** full face opening, scarf knot and tail on her left (screen right), satchel on her right.
- **Three-quarter:** turned towards screen left. The face opening shifts and narrows. The far arm and
  far leg are slimmer and drawn behind the body. The satchel peeks out on the far hip.
- **Side (left profile):** a crescent of face under the hood brim. The scarf tail hangs in front of the
  chest. The cloak trails behind. The satchel shows behind the hip.
- **Back:** hood, scarf wrap and mantle over the cloak. Arms, belt and tunic exist in the rig but the
  cloak covers them, and the longer right hem is on screen right.

## Rig

Twenty-one nodes. Every node has a stable id, a parent, and a pivot in every view (`harmonie-rig.ts`).

| Node                            | Parent    | Pivot (y) | Role                                           |
| ------------------------------- | --------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| `root`                          | none      | 800       | Ground point. Moves and turns the figure       |
| `pelvis`                        | `root`    | 490       | Centre of mass, bob and tilt                   |
| `torso`                         | `pelvis`  | 470       | Bends at the waist, scales for breathing       |
| `neck`                          | `torso`   | 218       | Base of the neck                               |
| `head`                          | `neck`    | 178       | Nods and turns                                 |
| `hood`                          | `head`    | 178       | Follows the head with its own lag              |
| `leftUpperArm`, `rightUpperArm` | `torso`   | 240       | Shoulders                                      |
| `leftForearm`, `rightForearm`   | upper arm | 398       | Elbows                                         |
| `leftHand`, `rightHand`         | forearm   | 548       | Wrists                                         |
| `leftThigh`, `rightThigh`       | `pelvis`  | 512       | Hips                                           |
| `leftShin`, `rightShin`         | thigh     | 636       | Knees                                          |
| `leftFoot`, `rightFoot`         | shin      | 758       | Ankles                                         |
| `cloak`                         | `torso`   | 222       | Hangs from the collar, lags for follow-through |
| `scarf`                         | `neck`    | 222       | Pivots at the knot on her left                 |
| `satchel`                       | `pelvis`  | 470       | Hangs from the belt, swings with lag           |

**Transforms.** Each node is a group at its pivot. Its local transform is its pivot minus its parent's
pivot, plus the pose's rotation, offset and scale. Parts are drawn relative to the pivot, so rotation
always happens at the joint. Limb parts are capsules whose round ends are centred on the joints,
which keeps joints closed at any angle.

**Rotation sign.** Degrees, clockwise on screen, relative to the parent. In the side rig, a positive
value swings a hanging part (arm, leg, cloak, scarf) forwards, and tilts an upright part (torso, neck,
head) backwards. Leaning forward is negative.

**Draw order is separate from the hierarchy.** A cloak hangs from the torso but must draw behind the
legs, which belong to the pelvis. A hierarchy cannot express that, so the scene schema gained an
optional node `depth`: within one root, nodes draw in depth order, and equal depths keep the old
hierarchy order. Scenes that set no depth draw exactly as before. This is how 2D skeletal rigs usually
separate bones from draw order. Each view has its own depths, so the cloak is behind everything from
the front and side, and in front of the body from the back.

**Inverse kinematics.** Legs are posed with two-bone IK when a pose is built, never during playback.
It accounts for pelvis tilt and lifts the ankle automatically when the toe is pointed or raised, so
the boot rests on the ground instead of sinking into it. Evaluation stays a blend of stored values.

**Supported motion.** Walk and run (legs, pelvis bob, arm swing), reach (arm chain, head, rise on
the toes), 2D body rotation (`root`, `pelvis`, `torso`), cloth follow-through (`cloak`, `scarf`,
`hood`, `satchel` on their own track), idle breathing (`torso` scale), and pose transitions (blends
between any two poses).

## Pose sheet

All poses use the side rig, at one scale.

| Pose              | Reads as                                                                        |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Neutral standing  | Upright, weight even, head slightly lowered: calm and observant                 |
| Walking           | Contact position, opposite arm swing, cloak and scarf trail                     |
| Running           | Push-off, forward lean, bent arms, cloth streams back                           |
| Reaching          | Up on the back toes, arm and gaze lead the line of action                       |
| Crouching         | Pelvis tilted, low and compact. The cloak bunches instead of hitting the ground |
| Defensive posture | Wide base, weight back, both forearms raised as a guard                         |
| Quiet, withdrawn  | Spine curls, head bowed, arms drawn in, feet together                           |

## Motion preview

24 fps. Character and cloth on twos. Four shots, 15 s in total, all keyed data:

| Shot    | Time      | Content                                                                                   |
| ------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `idle`  | 0–4 s     | Breathing at one breath per 2 s. The cloak settles about 4 frames later                   |
| `walk`  | 4–8 s     | Four 1-second cycles in place, 8 keys each. Cloth swings at step rate, 2 frames behind    |
| `reach` | 8–11.5 s  | Anticipation, reach up, settle, return. Camera pulled back to keep the hand in frame      |
| `cloth` | 11.5–15 s | Sharp lean and stop, then back. Cloak, scarf, hood and satchel drag, overshoot and settle |

Follow-through is deterministic because it is stored: the cloth track has its own keys, set later than
the body's. Nothing is simulated and there is no timer per part. The same time always gives the same
picture, and tests check this both in code and in the browser.

## Visual QA (version 1)

Checked in Chromium, from the generated SVG and from the running preview. Screenshots come from
`npm run capture` and go to `artifacts/screenshots/harmonie*` (not committed).

Defects found and fixed during the build:

1. The hood ended in a flat edge and flared out like a helmet. It now tapers into the neck.
2. The mantle read as square shoulder pads. It now has a natural shoulder slope and an A-line flare.
3. The legs were too thin against the cloak, so they were widened.
4. The back view had no shoulder structure. A mantle layer was added.
5. Teal sleeves vanished against the teal tunic in profile. They are now Deep Shadow.
6. In "crouching" the tunic stayed upright and the cloak went through the ground. Pelvis tilt and
   cloak bunching were added, and an IK bug found under tilt was fixed.
7. Pointed toes sank into the ground. IK now lifts the ankle by the needed amount.
8. The walk's up position floated, because a straight leg cannot reach that far. The pelvis bob was
   corrected, and a test now checks that a boot touches the ground on every key.
9. In the three-quarter view the mantle covered the scarf. The scarf now draws on top.
10. "Defensive" looked like a wave and "withdrawn" like sleepwalking. Both were re-posed.
11. The reach left the frame, so its shot was reframed.
12. On the sheet, labels collided and the running pose left its cell. Pose cells are now sized to each
    pose, and a test checks that every figure stays inside its cell.

Still open:

- **No person has watched the preview in motion.** Timing and weight are untested by eye.
- The front and three-quarter silhouettes are similar. The three-quarter turn reads closer to the
  front than a true 45°, so it could be pushed further.
- The walk is a cycle in place. Arms swing on a single upper-arm angle, and there is no overlap in the
  wrists yet.
- Cloth moves as rigid pieces: the cloak rotates and bunches but does not bend. Bending would need a
  cloak split into segments or mesh deformation, which is not built.
- The face crescent in profile is narrow. At small sizes it reads as part of the hood.
- Only the side view is rigged for animation. The other views are drawings on the same rig, at rest.
- Sheet text uses system fonts, so exact text width differs by machine. Layout has margins for this,
  but text is not part of the overlap test.
