# Screenwriting and emotional storytelling

**Status: guidance only (Planned).** No script exists, and future story data must not force one
structure. Sources: [references](../REFERENCES.md).

## Meaning

| Term                   | Meaning                                              |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Subject                | What the story is about, as a topic                  |
| Theme                  | The question or idea it keeps returning to           |
| Moral claim            | What the story seems to argue through its outcome    |
| Philosophical question | A question with strong answers on more than one side |

## Drive

- **Want vs need:** drama often lives in the gap between them.
- **Conflict and stakes:** what stands in the way, and what is concretely lost on failure?
- **Moral dilemmas:** the hardest choices are between two goods or two harms.
- **Choice and consequence:** characters reveal themselves by choosing under pressure, and the result
  should follow from the choice.

## Technique

- **Subtext:** people rarely say exactly what they mean. Let action carry what dialogue avoids.
- **Symbols and motifs:** repeated images gain meaning through context and change over time.
- **Setup and payoff:** plant something early enough that its return feels earned.
- **Point of view:** decide who the audience travels with and what they are allowed to know.
- **Dramatic irony:** the audience knows more than the character, creating tension.
- **Visual metaphor:** express an inner state through something that can be seen.

## Emotion

- **Attachment:** usually comes from understanding, wanting something with them, and seeing effort.
- **Vulnerability:** comes from what a character risks, more than from what happens to them.
- **Restraint:** holding back can feel stronger than showing everything. Leave room for the audience.

## Structure

Act models, sequences, cycles and non-linear forms are all tools. Choose what serves the story.

An ending usually feels meaningful when it answers the central question in a way that is both
surprising and, looking back, inevitable. It may be open, as long as that is a deliberate choice.
