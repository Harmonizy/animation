# Worldbuilding

**Status: guidance only (Planned).** This is a template with no lore. Sources:
[references](../REFERENCES.md).

## Causal principle

```text
World Rule -> Consequence -> Human Behavior -> Conflict -> Story
```

A fact earns its place when it changes what people do. If removing it changes nothing on screen, it is
background at most.

## Template

| Domain                 | Prompt                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Geography and climate  | What does the land make easy, hard or impossible?            |
| Ecology                | What lives here, what does it depend on, and what is scarce? |
| History                | Which past events are still visibly shaping the present?     |
| Politics               | Who decides, on what authority, and who disputes it?         |
| Economy                | What is valuable, who controls it, and who goes without?     |
| Culture and religion   | What do people treat as sacred, shameful or normal?          |
| Rules and consequences | What are the laws of this world, and what do they cost?      |
| Social hierarchy       | Who can say no to whom? How is rank visible?                 |
| Architecture           | How do climate, materials, wealth and power shape buildings? |

## Magic systems

Sanderson's essay relates how well an audience understands magic to how satisfyingly it can solve
problems. As a working guideline, not a rule:

- **Hard systems** have clear rules and costs, so they can fairly resolve conflict.
- **Soft systems** stay mysterious, and tend to serve wonder and atmosphere better than solutions.

## Environmental storytelling

Show history through evidence: wear, repairs, abandoned objects, paths worn by repeated use, and
buildings adapted for new purposes. Ask what happened here and what people do here every day.

## Validation questions for future data

- Does each rule list at least one consequence and one behavior it causes?
- Can each conflict be traced back to a rule?
- Can the important facts be shown in the environment rather than explained?
- Do the rules stay consistent, and is every exception paid for?
